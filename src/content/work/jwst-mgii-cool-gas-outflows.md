Galactic-scale outflows are a crucial component of galaxy evolution, yet their properties in the early Universe remain poorly constrained. We present the first statistical investigation of Mg II-traced cool gas outflows in galaxies spanning a wide cosmic timeline from z around 1 to z above 6 (with sample coverage extending to z around 10). Using thousands of public JWST/NIRSpec spectra, we employ a signal-to-noise-weighted spectral stacking technique on the Mg II 2796, 2803 absorption doublet.

We robustly detect blueshifted Mg II absorption in nearly all stellar mass and redshift bins, with the exception of the lowest-mass systems at z around 1-2. The outflow equivalent width exhibits a positive correlation with stellar mass (M*) at all epochs, with a fitted slope of 1.21 +/- 0.35.

Our work provides the first statistical constraints on Mg II-traced cool outflows in the low-mass (M* less than about 10^9.5 solar mass), high-redshift (z > 3) regime. We also find that outflow velocities generally remain below host halo escape velocities, consistent with a galactic fountain scenario. The consistency of the stellar-mass-outflow-equivalent-width relation across z around 2-6 suggests a persistent, unevolving feedback mechanism governing the baryon cycle toward cosmic dawn.
