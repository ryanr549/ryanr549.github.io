Stellar feedback is a key process regulating the baryon cycle and is thought to strongly redistribute baryonic material inside and outside dark matter halos (DMHs), but direct observational evidence has remained limited. Through stacking analyses of about 400,000 galaxy spectra from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), we detect star-formation-driven cool outflows in Mg II absorption. Assuming gravity is the only force acting on launched gas, our calculations show that outflows from low-mass galaxies (log M* < 10) can escape beyond their DMHs. This result is consistent with our circumgalactic medium (CGM) absorption measurements along galaxy minor axes using background quasars. Together, these findings provide indirect evidence that stellar feedback drives the low baryon retention fraction in low-mass halos, and suggest that baryonic processes within galaxies are physically connected to diffuse matter beyond the DMHs.
